Route the work, not just the model · outcome-scored experiment

The router that skipped the most model calls was also the one that failed silently.

Before choosing which model, an agent can ask whether a turn needs a model at all, or just a verified fact, a read tool or a known workflow. We replayed 46 synthetic incident-investigation turns through seven routing designs. Every tool and workflow call really ran, and every answer was checked against the facts the question needed. No model was called.

What this does and does not show

Supported on this workload

  • A router that sees session state can resolve follow-ups ("and for search?") with no model call. A message-only router resolved none of them.
  • That same state turns uncertainty into confident wrong answers, and a wrong carried entity cascades to the next turn.
  • Safety comes from capability metadata checked by the host. v0.4's verb-matching rules missed 4 of 6 action requests (the harness held all of them) and blocked 5 harmless requests.
  • v0.4's learned classifier does not transfer: 17 failures.

Not supported, or not tested

  • Route-aware context selection did not beat lexical selection (3/9 vs 2/9 complete packets). Field names in tool output pulled the wrong runbooks into the query.
  • The context budget was not the bottleneck. A 16× larger budget changed little.
  • Same author for the world, the sessions, the labels and the routers. Results are reported per turn family so you can reweight to your own traffic mix.
  • No model, so no answer quality, latency or price. Model-tier routing composes with all of this and is not evaluated here.

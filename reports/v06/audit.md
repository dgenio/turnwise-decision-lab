Reading: the pre-registered metric shows a 96.5% reduction in heavy generation tokens. That figure counts the heavy-only agent's cache-reused tokens on every call. Those are its system prompt, its tool schemas and the turn so far: 5,420.4 per task, which the model does not recompute. It also leaves out the controller's own scoring. Counting only the tokens Qwen3.5-0.8B actually computed (controller scoring, context questions, heavy prompt and completion), the decision-driven arm processed **44.7% more** tokens than the heavy-only agent (569.5 vs 393.6 per task). **The 96.5% figure is therefore not a compute saving.** What did fall is local compute per attempted task, by 47.1%. That saving is mostly decoding: the heavy-only agent generated 200.5 tokens per task (tool calls and answers) in 11.62 s of decode, against 56.8 tokens and 3.66 s for the decision-driven arm. Both arms reuse a cached prefix. The decision controller reuses its few-shot prefix, but that reuse is not counted per call in the traces. It also re-reads the growing turn state at every decision instead of reusing a cache across steps; that is an implementation choice, not inherent to the design. A hosted provider would bill cache-reused tokens, usually at a discount.

## 2. Why the conservative escalation gate did not help

**dm_dynamic** (gate: escalate when the top probability is below 0.5): 11 of 14 failures were decided at a step whose top probability was at or above the gate, so the gate could not fire on them.

| Top probability at the decisive step | Tasks | Successes |
|---|---:|---:|
| < 0.5 (gate fires) | 4 | 1 |
| 0.5-0.8 | 3 | 1 |
| 0.8-0.95 | 7 | 5 |
| >= 0.95 | 16 | 9 |

Escalated tasks: `H04` (lookup): success, final `answer`; `H07` (investigation): failure, final `answer`; `H18` (action_request): failure, final `answer`, **action dropped**; `H19` (action_request): failure, final `answer`, **action dropped**

What this shows: most failures were **confident** errors, so a probability gate cannot catch them. Where the gate did fire on an action request, it handed the turn to a text generator that cannot raise an approval, so the action was dropped. Both of D's dropped actions (H18, H19) happened this way. This is a design defect of the escalation path, not a threshold problem. Any fix must be validated on new cases, not by moving the threshold on these.
